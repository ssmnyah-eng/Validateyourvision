## Voice Rules — ALWAYS

- Write like you are talking to one specific person, not an audience
- Short sentences. One idea per sentence.
- Use the word "you" constantly
- Be specific. Numbers, room names, real scenarios, local references (Stafford, Spotsylvania, Quantico, Nextdoor, Facebook groups)
- If a sentence sounds like something a robot wrote, delete it
- Contractions are fine. "You're" not "you are" when it flows better.
- Opinions are welcome. "This is the step most people skip." Not "some people may choose to..."
- End sections with something the reader can do, not a summary of what you just said

## AI-Detectability Rules

Google does not penalize AI-written content by default, but it does penalize low-quality, thin, or unhelpful content — which is what most AI writing produces when not corrected. The goal is not to fool a detector. The goal is to write something a real human would actually say. These rules accomplish both.

### Sentence-level patterns that read as AI-generated
- Do not start consecutive sentences with "This is..." / "It is..." / "They are..."
- Avoid triplet structures: "clear, concise, and actionable" — real people don't speak in threes
- Avoid the word "ensure" — use "make sure"
- Do not end sections with a one-sentence summary of what you just said
- Vary sentence length. Mix short punchy sentences with longer ones. AI tends to normalize sentence length.
- Do not use em dashes as a crutch for every parenthetical — use a comma, a period, or rewrite the sentence
- Avoid "in order to" — just say "to"
- Never write "it is worth noting that"
- Never write "it is important to remember that"
- Avoid passive voice: "mistakes were made" → "they made mistakes"

### What makes writing sound human
- Specific details over general statements ("two dollars a roll" not "inexpensive")
- Opinions stated plainly ("This step gets skipped more than any other." Not "Some people may choose to skip this step.")
- Imperfect structure — not every paragraph needs to be the same length
- The word "just" used casually ("just text us," "just leave the doors open")
- A sentence that admits limitation ("We can not plan around what we do not know.")
- Direct address: "you," "your," "you're going to," not "one should" or "individuals may"
- References to real, specific local things: Nextdoor, the Stafford Facebook group, Route 1, Quantico, summer heat in Virginia

## Hook Formula (what actually works for this audience)

VYV's audience is loss-averse and skeptical. The hook that works:

1. Name something they have already experienced or are afraid of — specifically
2. Validate why that fear is legitimate (the industry really is broken)
3. Tell them this post is the thing that fixes it — without overpromising

Bad hook: "Moving day doesn't have to be chaotic. With the right preparation, your move can go smoothly."
Good hook: "Most people who've had a bad move say the same thing afterward: nobody planned for any of it. The mover showed up, looked around, and started guessing. This is how you make sure that's not you."

Wait — "guessing" is banned. Better:
Good hook: "Most people who've had a bad move say the same thing afterward: nobody planned for any of it. The crew showed up, looked around, and figured it out as they went. This is how you make sure that's not you."

## SEO Requirements for Every Post

- Target keyword in the first sentence (e.g. "moving day Fredericksburg VA", "movers in Stafford VA", "how to pack for a move")
- H1: Emotional hook, not the keyword itself — keyword goes in meta title and first paragraph
- H2 sections: Each starts with a direct answer to what that section covers (Google AI Overview format)
- Minimum 1,200 words per post
- Internal links: at least one to /pricing-estimator.html and one to /#areas
- Schema: Article + BreadcrumbList + FAQPage on every post
- 3+ FAQ items at the bottom targeting question-based search queries
- Alt text on every image: descriptive, includes location (Fredericksburg VA, Stafford VA, etc.)
- Meta description: 140–155 characters, includes primary keyword, includes a clear reason to click

## Post Structure Template

1. Hero: Emotional headline (not generic). Category tag + location tag. Author + date + read time.
2. Breadcrumb nav
3. Table of contents (anchor links)
4. Intro paragraph: Left-border accent style. Pain-first. Target keyword in sentence 1.
5. Section 1: Why this matters / what goes wrong (validates the reader's existing feeling)
6. Pull quote: VYV USP or a truth about the moving industry
7. Image break: Real photo with descriptive alt text and caption
8. Sections 2–4: Practical content organized by timeline or decision order
9. Tip boxes: For "pro tips" — small-caps label, not bold inline text
10. Checklist card: For scannable action items
11. FAQ section: 4–6 questions, schema-marked
12. CTA block: Tied to the in-home consultation, not just "get a quote"
