Moving day does not have to be chaotic. Whether you are moving within Fredericksburg, relocating to Stafford or Spotsylvania, or heading up to Northern Virginia, the difference between a smooth move and a stressful one almost always comes down to what you did in the days leading up to it. This guide gives you a room-by-room, day-by-day checklist so nothing gets left behind.

What to Do the Week Before Your Move

The week before your move is your most valuable window. This is when you can still fix problems before they become moving-day emergencies. Use each day intentionally.

Confirm everything with your moving company

If you are working with VYV, your move plan and start time are already set. Still, reach out a few days before to confirm the crew size, start time, and any last-minute additions to your inventory. If you bought new furniture, added a piano, or changed your destination address since the in-home consultation, your mover needs to know now.

Change your address before moving day

This one gets skipped constantly and causes weeks of headaches after the move. Set aside 30 minutes to update your address everywhere at once.

  • USPS mail forwarding at usps.com
  • Your employer's HR system for pay stubs and tax documents
  • Your bank, credit cards, and any financial accounts
  • Insurance providers: health, auto, renters or homeowners
  • Amazon, subscription boxes, and any recurring deliveries
  • Your doctor, dentist, and any pharmacy
  • Your children's school enrollment records

Schedule your utilities transfer

Contact your electricity, gas, water, and internet providers to schedule a service stop at your old address and a start date at your new one. Aim to overlap by one day so you are not stuck without power on move-in night.

Packing and Labeling the Right Way

The way you pack determines how fast your crew can work on moving day. Movers load efficiently when every box is sealed, labeled, and placed near the door. Here is how to pack smart, not just fast.

Label boxes by destination, not origin

Write the room in your new home on every box, not the room you are packing from. Your movers do not know your old layout, but they will walk your new home at the start of the day and learn it quickly. A box labeled "Primary Bedroom" is placed correctly every time. A box labeled "Mom's old office" is not.

Use a color-coding system

Pick one color of painter's tape per room. Put a strip on each box and a matching strip on the doorframe of that room in the new house. Your crew can sort boxes by color without reading a word. This alone shaves significant time off most moves.

Pack an "open first" box and keep it with you

This box does not go on the truck. It stays in your car or somewhere obvious and holds everything you need for your first 24 hours in the new home.

  • Phone chargers for every person in the household
  • One set of clothes and toiletries per person
  • Medications
  • Coffee maker and a few mugs
  • Snacks and a couple of bottles of water
  • Paper towels, toilet paper, and hand soap
  • A power strip and any remote controls
  • Bedding for the first night
Moving tip from VYV Aim to be fully packed at least two days before your move date. The last 10 percent of packing always takes longer than expected, and rushing the morning of creates confusion for your crew and slows down the whole job.

What to do with fragile items

Mark fragile boxes on the top and on at least two sides so your movers can see the label no matter how boxes are stacked. Use crumpled paper or bubble wrap to fill empty space in boxes with dishes or glassware — items should not shift when you tilt the box.

The Night Before Moving Day Checklist

Keep the night before simple. Your goal is to be ready so moving morning is just execution, not preparation.

Night before checklist

  • Do a final walkthrough of every room, closet, cabinet, drawer, and shelf
  • Check the attic, basement, garage, shed, and any outdoor storage
  • Defrost your refrigerator if it is going on the truck
  • Confirm your moving team's start time and have their contact number saved
  • Charge your phone and any devices you will need tomorrow
  • Set your "open first" box somewhere you will not miss it in the morning
  • Get to bed at a reasonable hour — fatigue is the most underrated moving day problem
Do not forget Defrost your refrigerator the night before if it is moving with you. A fridge that has been running until moving morning will have condensation and water in the drip tray. This leaks in the truck and is a real problem that slows everyone down.

Moving Day Morning: What to Do When Your Crew Arrives

When your movers arrive, they are ready to work immediately. Your job is to make sure they can. Here is the order that works best.

  1. Be awake and dressed at least 15 minutes before the scheduled start time.
  2. Clear a path from the main entrance to where your boxes and furniture are staged.
  3. Walk your crew through the home at the start. Point out anything fragile, high-value, or that needs special handling before anyone picks up a single box.
  4. Let your movers know the destination for each room in the new home so they can place items correctly from the start.
  5. Keep children and pets in one dedicated room, away from the work area. This protects everyone and lets your crew move without stopping every few minutes.
  6. Have water or drinks available for the crew, especially on warm days. VYV crews work efficiently through the summer heat in Fredericksburg, but staying hydrated is a shared responsibility.
  7. Do a final walkthrough of the old home before the truck pulls away. Check every room, every closet, every cabinet one more time.

What should stay in your car, not on the truck

  • Jewelry and other high-value items
  • Passports, birth certificates, Social Security cards, and other important documents
  • Medications and anything with a medical need
  • Anything truly irreplaceable: photographs, heirlooms, sentimental items
  • Your "open first" box
  • Laptop, tablet, and phone chargers
  • Your payment method for the final invoice

Common Moving Day Questions

How far in advance should I start preparing for a move in Fredericksburg, VA?

Start packing non-essentials two to three weeks out. Confirm logistics with your moving company one week before. Complete all packing two days before moving day. The more time you give yourself, the less stressful the final 48 hours will be.

What should I not put on the moving truck?

Keep valuables, important documents, medications, irreplaceable items, chargers, your "open first" box, and your payment method with you in your personal vehicle. These are things that cannot be replaced if lost or damaged, and they are items you will need immediately on arrival.

Do I need to tip movers in Fredericksburg?

Tipping is not required, but it is a genuine way to recognize a crew that worked hard. A common range is $20 to $50 per mover for a standard local move, and more for a long day, heavy specialty items, or difficult access. Cash on the day is the easiest way to do it.

How long does a typical local move take in the Fredericksburg area?

A two-bedroom home typically takes four to six hours with a professional crew. A three or four-bedroom home usually runs six to nine hours. Factors that add time include multiple flights of stairs, long carry distances, specialty items like pianos, and the amount of packing completed before the crew arrives.
